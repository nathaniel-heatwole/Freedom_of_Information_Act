SUMMARY / IMPACT

• Litigation consultant to McWatters v. ATF

• Goal: obtain audio and video records related to the Station Nightclub fire disaster (2003)

• Helped frame strategy, research evidence contradicting the government's theory, and secure affidavit from subject matter expert

• Wrote brief encapsulating public interest arguments supporting release of the records (FOIA.pdf)

• Successfully obtained 95% of the audio record – now seeking the entirety, as well as the video record

• Audio allowed next-of-kin of one victim to finally know his cause of death (smoke inhalation) – giving her great solace (22 years after the fire)

• Preparing article for peer-reviewed journal on contradictions in the application of near-death privacy by federal agencies and the experiences of our FOIA coalition

PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

• Causal factor in the occurrence of the disaster was inadequate ATF oversight of federally-licensed fireworks manufactures and suppliers and failure of federal controls on pyrotechnics

• ATF is the primary federal agency tasked with keeping these dangerous pyrotechnic devices away from non-licensed pyrotechnicians and out of venues such as the Station Nightclub

• ATF processes for detecting and preventing deliberate misuse of its systems by bad actors were either ineffective or nonexistent

• These records document (uniquely) the depths of the horrors that can result when the special trust placed in those government personnel who are tasked with fire safety and protection is violated

• The NIST investigation report on the fire made no recommendations directed at ATF, nor any recommendations related to federal controls of pyrotechnics (the heat source that caused the fire)

• Release of the records would encourage greater vigilance and proactivity by ATF and NIST, and the development of additional fire safety recommendations, policies, and preventative measures

PUBLIC SAFETY

• ATF used the audio record to understand the details of this particular fire event, but never for the wider purpose: creating generalizable knowledge to improve fire safety

• Audio contains information about survivability in fires and emergency egress behavior (both of which are key inputs to risk analysis, and in related risk management and policy-making)

• Overly conservative survivability criteria drain limited economic resources that could be better spent while inadequate safety margins expose the public to unacceptable risk

• By time-correlating the data on the audio to the parameters of the fire environment, the survivability criteria could be evaluated and their probabilistic component elucidated

• Put forth several direct quotes from the fire safety literature, saying the field does not anticipate ever having data like that contained on this audio record

PRIVACY CONCERNS

• Critically examines assumptions underlying doctrine of survivor's privacy: that it would cause pain to surviving family members (by reviewing empirical case studies of the impacts), and that transcripts are sufficient for public accountability purposes (by detailing the inherent limitations of transcripts)

• Examined the privacy and data protection practices used by federal agencies in other areas, revealing that the federal government has deemed these levels of uncertainty to be sufficient in other domains

• Uncovered contradictions in federal agency application of the survivor's privacy doctrine, by comparing the release outcomes for these records to those from other accidents and disaster events
